One Team Detail, every sport that has teams

Same exercise as the Player Detail study, same method: measure first, then build it rather than argue it. Every tab below renders the same twenty blocks from the same template, with no sport === 'x' in the renderer. The team side has two blocks the player page does not — a roster and a standings table — and it loses none.

What the measurement said — and it inverts the player-side answer

On the player side MLB turned out to be mid-table, with NFL and CFB carrying twice its stat vocabulary. On the team side the ordering flips. MLB has sixteen years of team rating history; every other sport has one season. And two sports have no team page at all — which is a fact about those sports, not a hole in the data.

Measured 2026-08-29 against team_elo_history, player_game_history and game_odds_book_lines. The Elo table covers exactly the six sports that have a team adapter — independent confirmation that the six/two split is real.

Tennis and golf are not thin. They have no teams.

All 271,964 tennis rows in player_game_history carry a null team_id — zero distinct teams, by construction. TeamDetailPanel already dispatches on exactly six sports, and neither tennis nor golf has a teamDetailAdapter.ts. This is the one case where CLAUDE.md §4's “render nothing” rule is exactly right: there is no page to render, so there should be no tab. For tennis the player page is the entity page. Golf's structural sibling is a tournament, not a team — the last tab shows the same twenty roles mapped onto a field and a course, to demonstrate the grammar carries.

The leak, and it is worse than the player side's

TeamDetailData.grades is typed TeamGrades | null and commented “NFL-only — MLB has no grading model, always null.” TeamGrades itself hardcodes nine NFL unit names — specialTeams, secondary, linebackers, dLine, passingOffense… No other sport can fill that shape, so no other sport gets grades. Meanwhile the NBA and NHL adapters emit no statGroups at all, so their team pages are the thinnest in the app today. One generic unitGrades list fixes both at once.

The six roles that carry the sport-specific content. units is the one doing the most work here — it is what replaces the nine hardcoded NFL grade fields.

What this means for the interface

  • grades: TeamGrades | null becomes unitGrades: UnitGrade[]. The nine hardcoded NFL fields become a list of { key, label, grade, rank }. NFL keeps all nine; MLB declares three (hitting, pitching, fielding); NHL declares four (offence, defence, power play, penalty kill). The chip row renders a list, so it never knows what a linebacker is. This also un-blanks the NBA and NHL team pages, which today emit no statGroups at all.
  • Two blocks are genuinely team-only: roster and standings. Neither has a player-page equivalent, and both are already in TeamDetailData today with their sport differences correctly data-driven (rosterSortByStats, rosterPageSize). That pattern is right — extend it rather than replace it.
  • Six sports, not eight, and that is the correct answer. Tennis has null team_id on every row; golf has no team concept. Do not build a tab that renders an apology. Golf's real sibling page is a tournament, shown here as the last tab because the grammar transfers cleanly — the field is the opponent unit, the course is the conditions block, the leaderboard is the standings.
  • The depth asymmetry is the opposite of the player page's, and it is about history. MLB has 78,550 Elo rows back to 2010; every other sport has a single season. The rating-history block is therefore the one place a sport genuinely renders less — and the honest fix is a shorter x-axis with the real span labelled, not a fabricated backfill.
  • Market coverage is the real gap to close before any of this ships. game_odds_book_lines has MLB on moneyline, total and spread, but NFL on moneyline only (72 rows) and CFB moneyline only. The splits grid and the price block both assume a spread. That is a data-sourcing task, exactly like tennis was on the player side.
